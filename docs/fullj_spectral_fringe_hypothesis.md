## 1. Why the power spike language may be misleading

The observable-facing tests used

\[

\]

A large midpoint/end-point power ratio can therefore arise if neighboring signed transfers lie close to zeros while the midpoint lies on a finite lobe. This is not by itself evidence for a pole or resonance.

The completed local data show exactly this pattern in several places.

At `z=0.2`, around the strongest W2 power violation,

\[
T(0.1625)\simeq +0.01912,\qquad
T(0.16375)\simeq -0.18683,\qquad
T(0.1650)\simeq +0.01908.
\]

Thus the reported power ratio near 95.5 is generated by two neighboring near-nodes surrounding a finite negative lobe. The signed response is therefore the primary mechanistic object.

Similarly, in W1,

\[
T(0.1000)\simeq +0.00617,\qquad
T(0.10125)\simeq +0.05515,\qquad
T(0.1025)\simeq -0.00182,
\]

and in W3,

\[
T(0.1950)\simeq -0.15323,\qquad
T(0.19625)\simeq -0.26360,\qquad
T(0.1975)\simeq +0.04459.
\]

The working interpretation is therefore `spectral lobe / node structure`, not `isolated divergent spike`.
